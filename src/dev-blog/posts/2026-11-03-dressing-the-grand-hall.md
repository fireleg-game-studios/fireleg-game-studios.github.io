The Grand Hall is one of the first big rooms you walk through in MANOR VERBIER. It has to tell you at a glance who lived here, and what kind of evening stopped dead in this house.

<div class="post-pair">{% figure "blog/hall-before.webp", "The Grand Hall before the work: a dark room with a checkerboard floor and almost no detail.", "Before: the prototype, from the same spot." %}{% figure "blog/hall-after.webp", "The Grand Hall after the work: columns, panelling, portraits, carpet and chandelier.", "After: the same view today." %}</div>

## The brief: aristocratic luxury

We wanted wealth you can read from across the room, built up in layers:

- **Walls:** dark oak panelling with carved quatrefoils, a gilded rail, and tall fields of tone-on-tone burgundy damask.
- **Columns:** seventeen engaged columns of red griotte marble, with gilded bases and acanthus capitals.
- **Ceiling:** a grid of oak beams, painted coffers with gilded rosettes, and a central medallion 4.6 metres across.
- **Floor:** pale stone set with black marble, crossed by wool runners.
- **Furniture:** consoles, mirrors, portraits, armchairs and urns, all kept close to the walls so that the room stays easy to cross when you are in a hurry.

{% figure "games/manor-verbier/chandelier.webp", "The great chandelier hanging from its medallion in the dark hall.", "The chandelier: 3.4 metres wide, three tiers, 36 candles." %}

## Light does most of the work

The chandelier carries the shadows and is visible in the air around it. A soft fill keeps the room readable, a few warm glows lift the walls, and a cold shaft of moonlight enters through the great door. That is eight lights in total, and only one of them casts shadows. Keeping that number low is how a room this rich stays smooth to play.

## Then we damaged it

A perfect room would feel like a showroom. So we added dust along the walls, small cobwebs in the corners, patina on the gold, and wear down the middle of the carpet, where people actually walked.

## What we were not allowed to change

Art never gets to move the game. The 24 steps of the grand staircase kept their exact position, slope and dimensions, and a check confirms them one by one. The new oak casing, brass nosing and carpet are all built around them.

We also made one decision by leaving something out: this room has no decorative clock. Clocks matter in this game, and a fake one on the wall would send you the wrong way.
