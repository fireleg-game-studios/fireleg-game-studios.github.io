Atmosphere is not one feature. It is a hundred small decisions that only work when they agree with each other. Here is how we approach it in MANOR VERBIER.

## Every room remembers the night of the ball

At one point, all of the manor's sixty rooms shared the same mauve wall and the same black skirting. Today each one has its own walls, floor, ceiling and light.

Most rooms also hold a small moment: an object, its light and its damage, telling a piece of that evening. A fallen chandelier with two candles still burning. A leak and its bucket. A broken window with snow drifting in. A table set for a meal nobody finished.

## Each level has its own air

Dust hangs in the air, and you only see it where light catches it: in your lamp, in a shaft of moonlight, around a candle. Mist is measured out floor by floor, and each level ages in its own way, with saltpetre in the cellars, old tobacco in the apartments and leaks in the attic.

{% figure "blog/cellars.webp", "A cellar corridor of old brick and stone, by torchlight.", "The cellars, by torchlight." %}

## Sound you can navigate by

The same logic applies to what you hear. Each level has its own ambience: dripping water under the vaults of the cellars, wind on the tall windows of the ground floor, creaking parquet and a ticking clock in the apartments, wind between the tiles of the attic.

Footsteps never repeat the same take twice in a row, and sounds are muffled by the walls and closed doors between you and their source. Many of them, the ambient loops included, are created by us rather than taken from a library.
