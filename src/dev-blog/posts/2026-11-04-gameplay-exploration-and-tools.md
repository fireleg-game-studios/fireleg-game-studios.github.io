MANOR VERBIER gives you very few things. We wanted each of them to be useful, and each of them to cost you something. This is how the game plays today, and all of it may still change.

## The lamp

Your lamp runs on batteries, and spare ones have to be found. It is your main way of reading a dark room, and you hold it in your own hand: look down and you see your arm, run and it swings with you.

## Noise

Moving fast is loud. Running is the quickest way out of trouble and the quickest way into it, while soft steps are slow and almost silent. Footsteps also change with the floor beneath you, so the house always reports where you are.

If you prefer to play with the sound low, or cannot hear it, the game can show captions for noises around you, including the direction of footsteps.

## Hiding

Each level has its own hiding places. They are not a safe room: sooner or later, you have to step out again. And if you are caught, time rewinds to the last clock you wound. What you have already done is what you carry forward.

## The notebook

Everything you read goes somewhere. The list of objectives shows your current step and what follows, and the notebook gives you the whole picture, ticked off as you go.

## The map, in your hands

The map is a real object in the world. Open it and your character raises a sheet of old parchment with both hands, and it unfolds in front of you. It never replaces your view:

- **You keep moving,** at a slow walk. You cannot run or jump while reading.
- **You can still look around.** Your head turns about 34 degrees either way before your body follows, so you can read and watch the corridor at once.
- **The compass tells the truth.** It is a modelled brass instrument, and its needle points to the real north of the house.
- **The paper only knows what you know.** A room you have visited is hatched and named. A room you have only seen from its door is hatched, with no name. Everything else does not exist on the sheet yet.

The plan itself is not drawn by hand: it is computed from the game's own layout, down to the doors and the furniture, so the map can never disagree with the house.
